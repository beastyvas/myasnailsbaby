Pick **Gel-X** if you want length and shape quickly with a lighter, flexible feel. Pick **acrylic** if you want a hard, sturdy set that takes extreme shapes or a lot of nail art. Pick **structure gel** (often called builder gel) if you like your own length and just want your natural nails to look polished and feel a bit tougher. All three are good options. The right one depends on your nails, your hands and how you live in them.

Here's how they compare, what each one is like to wear, and some questions to help you decide.

## The quick comparison

| | Gel-X | Acrylic | Structure gel (builder gel) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Pre-shaped soft-gel tips bonded with gel and cured under a lamp | Liquid monomer mixed with powder polymer that hardens in the air | A thicker gel layered over your natural nail and cured under a lamp |
| Adds length? | Yes, from short to long | Yes, including very long and dramatic shapes | Not really. It works with your natural length |
| How it feels | Light and a little flexible | Hard and rigid | Light, closer to your own nail |
| Smell during the service | Low | Noticeable, from the liquid | Low |
| How it comes off | Soaked off in acetone | Soaked off in acetone, often after filing down | Soaked off in acetone |
| Good for | Fast, even length; clean everyday sets | Heavy wear, bold shapes, 3D art | Short to medium natural nails that bend or snap |

## Gel-X: length without the bulk

Gel-X is a brand of soft-gel extension made by Aprés. Your tech fits a full-cover gel tip to each nail, then bonds it with gel cured under a lamp. Aprés says the tips usually wear for three to four weeks when they're applied correctly. Because every tip comes pre-shaped, the length and curve can look very even from finger to finger.

It feels lighter than acrylic, and the soft gel has a little give. The trade-off is that it's less rigid. If you're very hard on your hands, a lot of typing, lifting or opening things, you may notice it more than you would with acrylic. The full breakdown of what makes a Gel-X set last, or pop off early, is in our guide on how long Gel-X lasts.

## Acrylic: the strongest build

Acrylic is made by mixing a liquid monomer with a powder polymer. The bead hardens in the air as your tech shapes it. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) describes acrylic nails as made mostly of acrylic polymers, and notes that ethyl methacrylate is one of the monomers used. The FDA also points out that some people can become allergic to it, which is why it's meant to stay off your skin.

Acrylic is hard and rigid. That makes it a good fit for long lengths, sharp shapes like stiletto, and heavier nail art. It does have a noticeable smell while it's being applied. The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) recommends good ventilation in nail salons for exactly this kind of product.

The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) notes that acrylic application usually means filing the surface of the natural nail rough so the product grips, and that this filing thins the nail. Good technique keeps that filing light, but it's worth knowing.

## Structure gel: your own nails, but sturdier

Structure gel goes by a few names: builder gel, structured manicure, gel overlay. It's a thicker gel brushed over your natural nail and shaped with a gentle curve, called an apex, before it's cured. NailPro, a trade magazine for nail professionals, describes a structured overlay as a way to add strength and shape to the natural nail without adding length.

It suits people who like their own length and want a manicure that looks smooth and feels a little more solid than gel polish alone. If your nails tend to bend or snap at the corners, the extra layer can help them hold up to daily life. It isn't the right choice if you want much more length than you have now. That's what Gel-X and acrylic are for.

## Questions that make the choice easier

Ask yourself these before you book:

1. **How much length do you want?** Your own length points to structure gel. Short to long, with even shapes, points to Gel-X. Very long or dramatic points to acrylic.
2. **How hard are you on your hands?** Lots of lifting, gym time or hands-on work leans toward acrylic's rigidity. Desk work and everyday life suit all three.
3. **How do you feel about the smell?** If strong product smells bother you, Gel-X or structure gel is the easier appointment.
4. **What nail art do you want?** 3D charms and heavy designs sit well on acrylic. Most hand-painted art works on all three.
5. **How are your natural nails doing?** If they're thin, peeling or sore, talk to your tech first. A break or a gentler option may make more sense right now.

## Things to know whichever you choose

- **Soak off, don't peel.** The AAD warns that picking or peeling off gel and artificial nails can damage the natural nail, and recommends having them removed instead.
- **Every enhancement asks something of your nails.** The AAD says gel nails can leave nails brittle, peeling or cracked, and suggests breaks so nails can recover. Taking a few weeks off between sets is always fine.
- **Desert life is hard on hands.** Henderson's dry air and long pool season dry out skin and cuticles. The AAD suggests moisturizing after you wash your hands and wearing gloves for dishes and cleaning, whatever set you're wearing.
- **Sunscreen before the lamp.** Gel-X and structure gel cure under a lamp. The AAD recommends a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on your hands beforehand.

## When it's not a nail-tech question

If a nail is painful, swollen, lifting with green, yellow or dark color under it, or the skin around it is red or itchy after a set, see a doctor or podiatrist. A nail tech won't put product over a nail like that, and none of these three sets is a fix for it. Choose your next set once you've been checked.
