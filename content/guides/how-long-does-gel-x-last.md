A Gel-X set usually lasts about three to four weeks. Aprés, the company that makes Gel-X, says its tips wear for 3–4 weeks when they're applied correctly. How close you get to that depends on two things: how the set was put on, and how your hands spend those weeks. Prep, fit and curing are on your nail tech. Water, heat, picking and using your nails as tools are on you.

Here's what usually shortens a set, what helps it last, and when it's time to come back in.

## What Gel-X is, in one minute

Gel-X is a brand of soft-gel extension. Instead of sculpting length out of product, your tech picks a pre-shaped, full-cover gel tip for each nail, fits it, and bonds it to your natural nail with gel that cures under a lamp. Because the whole tip is gel, it soaks off in acetone when it's time for removal.

That bond is why prep matters so much. The tip only holds as well as the surface underneath it.

## Why a Gel-X set pops off early

When a nail lifts or pops off in the first week or two, it's almost always one of these.

| What happened | Why it shortens wear | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oils or lotion left on the nail before the set | Gel bonds poorly to an oily surface | Skip hand cream the morning of your appointment; your tech preps and cleans each nail |
| Tip didn't fit the nail edge to edge | Gaps at the sides leave weak spots that catch and peel | Careful sizing of each tip, filed to your nail shape |
| Gel not fully cured | Under-cured gel is softer and lets go sooner | Curing for the full time the lamp's instructions call for |
| Lots of time in water | The AAD says too much water exposure weakens nails and can make them split, peel or break | Gloves for dishes and cleaning |
| Using nails as tools | The AAD says this can chip or split nails and lift the nail | Use a key, a coin or the pad of your finger instead |
| Picking at a lifted edge | It pulls up more of the set, and the AAD warns picking can damage your nails | Leave it and book a removal or a fix |
| Length you aren't used to yet | Long nails take more knocks while you adjust | Start with a shorter length and go up next time |

## Living in the desert with a fresh set

Henderson and the rest of the southeast valley are dry, hot and sunny for much of the year. That changes how your hands feel more than how gel behaves.

- **Dry air and AC dry out skin and cuticles.** The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) suggests moisturizing right after you wash your hands. A little cuticle oil or petroleum jelly at bedtime keeps the skin around your set soft, so you're less tempted to pick.
- **Pool season means more time in water.** The AAD links heavy water exposure with weaker nails. Swimming isn't off limits, but dry your hands well afterward and give your cuticles some oil.
- **Sun on your hands.** The AAD recommends putting a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on your hands before a gel manicure. That's good advice in Henderson any day of the week.
- **Hard water and cleaning.** Lots of hand washing, dishes and household cleaners all add up. Gloves for wet chores protect your nails and your set.

## An aftercare routine that helps a set last

**The first day**
- Give your hands a break from very hot water, like long baths or the hot tub.
- Don't test the tips by flicking or pressing on the edges.

**Every day**
- Wear gloves for dishes and cleaning.
- Moisturize after washing your hands, and oil your cuticles before bed.
- Open cans, stickers and packages with something other than your nails.
- Leave your cuticles alone. The AAD says cuticles protect the nail root and advises against cutting or pushing them back yourself.

**Once a week**
- Look closely at each nail, especially the sides and the free edge.
- If one corner is starting to lift, don't glue it, file it or pull it. Book in.

## When to come back in

Most people come back around the three-to-four-week mark. By then your natural nail has grown out, the set sits further from your cuticle, and the balance of the nail shifts toward the tip. That makes it easier to snag.

Come in sooner if:
- a nail has lifted or popped off,
- an edge keeps catching on your hair or clothes,
- the grow-out bothers you.

## Removal: soak, don't peel

When it's time to take a set off, peeling or prying it off is the fastest way to end up with thin, sore nails. The AAD calls acetone the most effective way to remove gel and warns that picking at gel or filing it off can do real damage. If a set is lifting, the AAD's advice is to see a manicurist and have it removed.

A professional soak-off removal takes the gel down gently, so your natural nails are ready for the next set or a break.

## When to see a doctor instead

Some changes aren't a nail-tech issue. If you notice pain, swelling, redness around the nail, a green, yellow or dark color under a lifted nail, or a nail separating from the skin with no clear reason, see a doctor or podiatrist. A nail tech won't apply product over a nail like that. The new set can wait until you've been checked.

The AAD also points out that gel manicures can leave nails brittle, peeling or cracked, and suggests saving them for special occasions if your nails are struggling. Taking a break between sets is always an option.

## A quick word on lamps

Gel needs a lamp to cure. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) considers nail curing lamps low risk when they're used as the label directs, and says to follow the exposure times in the lamp's instructions. Sunscreen on your hands beforehand, as the AAD suggests, is an easy extra step.

## The short version

- Gel-X usually lasts about three to four weeks.
- Good prep, a close fit and a full cure set it up to last.
- Water, picking and using your nails as tools cut it short.
- In the desert, moisturize, wear gloves for wet chores and put sunscreen on your hands.
- Lifting? Book a fix or a removal. Don't pull it off.
